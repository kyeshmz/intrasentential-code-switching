1. Start with what the reader needs to know in {language}. Put actions in an order that makes sense. Introduce a file, place, or person before referring back to it.
2. Keep {language} as the grammatical base. Choose an English phrase the audience might actually use and attach it using the surrounding language's own grammar. Do not count a name, acronym, or isolated English word as a switch.
3. Keep the meaning exact. Preserve timing, quantities, conditions, and what each term refers to. Translate part of an English term only if the remainder is still a natural phrase and the instruction is unchanged.
4. Match the reader's relationship to the writer. Skip filler, and repeat a useful term rather than renaming the same thing. Vary sentence length and shape as in a real message, and join a reason to its action when they belong together instead of giving every fact its own sentence of similar length. Do not invent details or add fake mistakes to sound casual.
5. Before returning the draft, read it as the recipient. Check that the action and conditions are clear and that every sentence has a natural switch. If an English phrase sounds forced, move the switch rather than adding a throwaway phrase.
